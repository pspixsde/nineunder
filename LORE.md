## Setting

The world resembles **medieval times** in broad strokes, but sits on its **own map** with its own geography and history.

**Life and nature** — Many creatures, races, animals, and plants populate it. Some are infused with magic; others are magical in themselves.

**Politics** — Multiple **dynasties and countries** vie for power. They are violent and hard to survive under; existence is rarely comfortable.

**Magic** — Magic is **common knowledge** but **not common practice**. Only certain people can wield it: those **born with the gift**, or those who rely on **magical items** and similar sources. (Acquisition through other means may exist; most folk never see the full picture.)

**The “pure”** — People with any **innate** tie to magic are called **pure**. They are treated as a **distinct people** in law, rumor, and custom, even though they are **not** morphologically or anatomically different from anyone else.

**Cursed places** — There are **cursed regions**: some hoard magical relics, some radiate a cursed aura, some are intrinsically magical from within. The varieties multiply; commoners lump them together as “bad ground.”

**What people think magic is** — Layfolk mostly know **two** names: **pure magic** (associated with the born-talented) and **dark magic** (whispers, fear, almost no reliable detail).

**Practitioners** — The world knows **sorcerers, warlocks, mages, druids**, and similar paths. There are **no** paladin orders, no widely trusted “pure-hearted” institutions, and no castes defined by virtue. **Everyone with power is at least self-interested**; altruism, when it appears, is personal, not systemic.

**Tone** — The world is **harsh and unforgiving**. **Life expectancy is low.**

## The Dread Pit

**The Dread Pit** is this setting’s answer to **hell or purgatory**: a prison of suffering and broken minds.

When **mortals** arrive there, they are **fated** to infinite torture. There is no heaven after the Pit — only lack of existence. The Dread Pit keeps the souls of the arrived intact so that torture may continue.

The Pit has **many dwellers** and is said to contain **nine circles** (structure and geography to be expanded later).

### The Nine Arenas (draft)

The Dread Pit is currently conceived as **nine arenas**. Details are still in progress; the entries below are the first established notes.

#### 1. Arena I — The Grey Threshold

**Atmosphere & geography** — The first arena exists in a state of permanent, stagnant dawn. The sky is perpetually caught in the moment just before sunrise — slightly brighter than pitch black, but the sun never ascends. The entire arena is a massive, isolated island-platform enveloped in dense mist and bordered by **Ink**.

One side of the platform forms a distinct shore against the river **Inkin** (also known as **Sinner Man's River**): a vast body of **Ink** so wide that the opposite bank is entirely invisible. Even the most hardened, ancient denizens of the Pit feel an absolute, paralyzing terror at the thought of entering it.

On the **opposite side** of the floating arena lies **Sun-Glint Vale**. It is a green, beautiful valley with fresh grass and a bright sky — separated from the main arena by a thick, layered wall of cobblestone, brick, and something that resembles bone. Small openings in the wall are barred like windows; through them the vale seems as distant as a dream, yet enough of its beauty shows through. In the middle of the wall stands a great closed **gate** that no one can pass. The far end of the valley is not visible; it is the closest thing to heaven that residents of the Pit can ever see.

Rumors and half-seen visions suggest **unbaptized infants drifting overhead** (TBD: whether this is literal, symbolic, or both).

**Environmental hazards** — The arena is dotted with pools and cracked structures containing stagnant ink fed from the river. Because these pools are disconnected from the main current, their potency is diluted, but they remain highly hazardous. Mere contact with this sluggish, dark fluid saps a being's strength, slows movement, and infuses the mind with acute, unnatural dread. The effect lingers briefly even after one steps free.

This is the game’s **start location**: relatively small, suited to onboarding and early orientation (tutorial and preparation beats TBD).

**Vareth and the gate** — As the protagonist arrives in the Pit, they hear a voice and recognize it almost immediately: **Vareth**. In the world of the living, Vareth is treated like a children’s boogeyman. Myth says he was the **first sinner**, one whose crimes — often tied to dark magic — were so severe that the universe did not wait for death: he was dragged into the Pit **alive**.

In the Pit, he is real and present, and his reputation is contradictory. The protagonist does not know whether he serves the Pit, governs part of it, or suffers under it like everyone else. They only know one thing with certainty: **something is wrong about their arrival**, and Vareth is tied to it.

When the arena is **cleared of all its enemies**, Vareth opens the gate. Crossing it, the vale dissolves like a mirage into an empty continuation of the arena’s ground — a narrow path that leads to what seems like a cliff. **Ink** pours down from within the ground like a waterfall, as vast and dreadful as Inkin itself. The protagonist must endure the liquid, fight the fear, and pass through it to reach the next arena: weakened, but alive.

#### 2. Arena II — Obsession and Madness

Weather shifts constantly and violently, but most of it is unreal — hallucinated climate laid over real torment. The exception is the wind: sudden blasts that punch into tortured bodies, always catching them off guard before rising into hurricane force.

In gameplay terms, this wind manifests as a roaming hazard: a literal moving force that sweeps through the map, drags entities toward it, and deals heavy damage.

Visual tone: mostly dark, with muted palettes and sickly organic textures — brown-white brain-like mass, rot, and damp flesh.

## Characters

All **playable characters** begin in **the Dread Pit**, each for their own reason. Their stories converge where the game starts.

### Undead Hunter

**Summary** — A hunter who lived by honest work and fair dealing, cast out for **rumors of dark magic in his blood**. He built a quiet life at the forest’s edge until a **cursed beast** and **human cruelty** destroyed everything he loved. He died with a vow of vengeance — and woke in the Pit, **half-dead**, **dulled**, and **hungry** in ways that no longer feel entirely human.

**Backstory**

He was a **hard-working hunter**: tough but fair, providing for his family. **Local rumor** painted him as heir to **dark magic**; his people were **ostracized**. He **moved deep into remote woods** and built a **house with his own hands**, hoping distance would keep his **wife and son** safe.

For a time, life was **quiet**.

One hunt, he tracked a **huge black boar**. It would feed them for a long while. He **lined up his best shot** and struck it **square in the head**. The beast **only staggered**. Half its face was **torn away**; the **remaining eye** burned **red**; its **tusks lengthened and sharpened**; a **dark glow** clung to it. **Magic**, he knew — and he **turned to run**. **Too late.** The boar **gored** him. He **fought** until strength failed; his sight **narrowed**; he believed he was **dying**.

He **woke** in **cold sweat**, skull **splitting**, **voices** pressing from every direction. **Night**. **Wounded**, he **crawled** toward home, **weakening** with every length of ground, **clinging** to the thought of his family.

When he reached the house, **dread** struck before he crossed the threshold. The **door** was **ripped** open; the **rooms** **trashed**; **glass** **shattered**. **Not an animal** — **people**. He **staggered** inside anyway.

By the **bedroom** he saw a **foot** and **dared to hope**. **Hope ended there.** He found **his wife and his son** **dead**. He was **sure** he would **follow** — but in that moment he **swore**: **whatever it took**, **however long**, he would **avenge** them.

He **collapsed** over their bodies and **died**.

**After** — His next awareness is **the Dread Pit**. He is **changed**: clothes **reduced to ash**, skin **half-dead**, **feelings** **muted**. Little remains in his mind but **bloodlust** or **emptiness**; almost nothing feels **human** as it once did.

**From here, the game begins.**

## Enemy types

- **Imp** — The oldest and weakest residents of the Dread Pit. Once ordinary mortal souls, they have been tortured and corrupted for so many eons that absolutely nothing human remains. Their inner anatomy is completely ruined; despite the Pit’s unnatural power to preserve a soul’s shape for continuous torment, their entrails have dissolved into a black, abstract sludge resembling rotten blood. Their mental state is completely severed — so warped that they can no longer comprehend the purpose or consequence of their existence. Now reduced to mindless bottom-feeders, they aimlessly roam the wastes, violently vomiting their corrosive insides at any passersby.
